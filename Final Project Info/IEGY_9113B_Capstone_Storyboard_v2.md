IE-GY 9113B — Storyboard: how to read and use it

PURPOSE

The capstone spine. It maps the 14 lessons to the final board presentation so students see the end state from Week
1, and each week's homework produces a defined piece of the final deck. It also serves as the deliverables-to-project
map: every graded deliverable is tagged to its origin — in-class work, homework/teamwork, or lab.

THE THREE PHASES = EXISTING MILESTONES (not a new framework)

Diagnose → Design → Implement is the plain-language gloss on the course's existing Milestones 1–2–3: M1 'What
is the problem?' (W1–W6, Labs 1–2), M2 'What is the design to close it?' (W7–W10, Lab 3), M3 'How do we manage
what we built?' (W11–W14, Lab 4 + final). No new construct was invented.

BOARD-DECK SIZING

Target ~13 content slides for the 20-minute board presentation (~60–90 sec each), plus 5 min Q&A. The slide count
mirrors the 7 Board-Memo sections so the deck and the memo are the same argument. Many lessons feed one slide
— the storyboard maps lessons-to-slides many-to-one, never one slide per lesson. Choosing what earns a slide is
itself the board discipline students practise.

TWO DIFFERENT THREE-PART STRUCTURES — DON'T CONFLATE

• Project method (this storyboard's spine): Diagnose → Design → Implement — how the TEAM works through the
semester.

• Roadmap (inside Milestone 3, slides S10–S11): Stabilise → Automate → Orchestrate — what the COMPANY does
over 36 months.
