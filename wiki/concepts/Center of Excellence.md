# Center of Excellence

A small, permanent team that owns **how** automation is done across a company. It governs and enables rather than building every bot itself. It sits between the business (which supplies demand and process knowledge) and IT (which supplies platform, identity, and security guardrails).

**Introduced:** [[W05_ScalingRPA_CoE_StudyNote_v2|Week 5 study note]]. Summary: [[Week-5-Scaling-RPA-and-CoE]].

## Five functions

| Function | Job | Failure it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| Governance and standards | Who may build, how, and how a bot is approved before production | Bot sprawl |
| Intake and review | A sanctioned front door: triage, then build centrally, hand to a supervised builder, or decline | Shadow IT |
| Ownership and monitoring | Named owner and a watched pulse for every bot, plus an audit trail | Silent errors running for weeks |
| Enablement and training | Turns willing business people into supervised builders | Scarcity bottleneck |
| Infrastructure and platform | Owns the plant and scales it with the programme | Infrastructure brittleness |

## Ideas to carry

- **Accountability is relocated, not removed.** The CoE is where it comes to rest.
- **Scale mindset:** remove the dam, install guardrails. The CoE is a steward, not a factory.
- **Hard to copy.** Bots can be bought. The people, standards, review pipeline, and accumulated judgment cannot.
- **Citizen development** is a complement that only works under CoE governance.
- Telefonica O2 ran 160+ robots with 3 reassigned Back-Office builders, so the idea holds at small scale too. At a 50-person firm the CoE may be one person and a written rule.
