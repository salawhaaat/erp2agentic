# Leverage Gap

The distance between a backbone that works technically and a backbone that delivers business value. Closing it, layer by layer (integrate → automate → make intelligent → make agentic), is the work of the whole course.

**Introduced:** [[W01_IntegrationBackbone_StudyNote_PB14|Week 1]], as the hand-off question to Week 2: "why does a backbone that works technically so often fail to deliver the value the business expected of it?"

**Named and diagnosed in full:** [[W02_FailureToLeverage_StudyNote_v6|Week 2 study note]] — the two cases, Nike's ~$100M supply-chain meltdown (2000) and Birmingham's Oracle go-live failure (2023), both automated before they obliterated. The cure: [[Obliterate Then Automate]].

**Used in:**
- [[W04_RPA_StudyNote|Week 4 study note]] — RPA is framed explicitly as "the first rung, not the whole climb" toward closing the leverage gap.
- [[Context-and-Diagnosis-Brief-Sweetgreen|Context-and-diagnosis brief (Sweetgreen)]] and [[RPA-Candidate-Scan-Sweetgreen|RPA-candidate scan]] — every diagnosis in this capstone is ultimately an argument about where Sweetgreen's leverage gap is and what closes it.

**Why it matters for the capstone:** the whole board memo is structured around this gap — §2/§3 diagnose it, §4 (RPA) begins to close it, and later sections (intelligent automation, agents) close it further. A deliverable that doesn't connect back to the leverage gap hasn't yet said why the board should care.
