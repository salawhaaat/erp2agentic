# Bailey Hydraulics

The Week 4 supporting case for RPA, and the course's second archetype made concrete: a mid-market manufacturer of mobile hydraulic components, running its business on NetSuite.

**Before:** a heavily customized legacy ERP with a web storefront bolted on separately — orders moved by hand between the website and the back-end system (the human as integration again), financial close dragging to twenty days.

**After:** consolidating onto one platform gave the order-to-cash flow a single source of truth. Order placement and fulfilment became effectively continuous inside the system; the financial close fell from twenty days to three. This is [[Obliterate Then Automate|traditional/native integration]], not RPA.

**Why it's in the wiki:** the working-backbone baseline against which an [[RPA Candidate Criteria|RPA-candidate scan]] should be run — before scanning a process for bots, ask what a working backbone's native integration has already made unnecessary. See [[RPA-Candidate-Scan-Sweetgreen]] for this reasoning applied to Sweetgreen.

**Source:** Oracle NetSuite, "Bailey Hydraulics pumps up ecommerce business with NetSuite."
