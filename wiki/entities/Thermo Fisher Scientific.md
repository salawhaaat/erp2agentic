# Thermo Fisher Scientific

The Week 4 primary case for RPA. One of the world's largest suppliers of scientific instruments and reagents, running on a sprawl of disparate ERPs, CRMs, and middleware after years of mergers. Handles more than ten million transactions and interactions a year across order entry, sales, and customer service.

**The problem:** systems were never integrated, so the human was the integration — a customer-care rep answering a simple question sometimes navigated nine different systems, and a routine call could run past ten minutes.

**Project Northstar:** built discrete modules on top of the existing sprawl instead of a months-long ERP consolidation — an order-entry automation combining RPA, machine learning, and OCR; a case-management module; a unified search interface; a customer-insights layer. Exceptions were routed to case management rather than forced through the bot.

**Result:** call hold times fell about 27% after full deployment ([[Three Threads|Customer thread]]); faster order processing accelerated cash flow ([[Three Threads|Cash thread]]).

**Used in:** [[W04_RPA_StudyNote|Week 4 study note]] as the anchored exemplar for [[RPA Candidate Criteria]] — the repetitive test ("order entry across millions of transactions a year").

**Source:** Thor Olavsrud, "Thermo Fisher transforms its customer experience," CIO, 12 Aug 2022.
