# Assessment Rubrics

NEW YORK UNIVERSITY TANDON SCHOOL OF ENGINEERING
Department of Technology Management and Innovation
IE-GY 9113B — Systems Integration: From ERP to Agentic AI

Fall 2026 · Companion reference to the course syllabus

These rubrics apply to the six graded components of IE-GY 9113B.

## How these rubrics work

Each criterion below is scored on five levels — Unacceptable (0%), Minimal (70%), Competent (85%), Effective (90%), and Mastery (100%). A component's score is the weighted sum of its criteria; component scores then combine according to the grade breakdown and map to the letter scale, both shown on this page. Five principles run through every rubric:

1. Reasoning is the primary graded objective. Across quizzes, discussion forums, in-class discussion, and the final presentation, marks are earned by explaining and defending the reasoning behind a business decision — why this diagnosis, why this design, why this trade-off — not by summarising a reading or reciting a framework.
2. Arguments, not summaries. Every written and spoken deliverable is assessed as a business argument: a claim, the evidence for it, and a defensible conclusion an executive could act on.
3. Cases and frameworks must do work. Claims are grounded in specific course cases and named frameworks, used to advance the argument rather than as decoration.
4. Platform-neutral. Work derived from the labs is graded on business outcomes, governance thinking, and solution-design quality — never on the platform chosen or its complexity. The Default no-code stack earns full marks.
5. Synthetic data only. All project data is synthetic; no confidential company information is used or required. Governance and risk thinking are assessed on the quality of the design, not on access to real data.

## 1 · Quizzes (20% — 10% each)

The two quizzes are analytical. Your Midterm (Week 6) covers Modules 1–2; your Final (Week 13) covers Modules 3–4 and adds one integrative question spanning the course. Each is administered on Brightspace with a 48-hour window; expect 400–600 words per question, written as a business argument rather than a summary and citing specific cases and frameworks. Each quiz closes with a 1:1 discussion with faculty to deepen your answers.

Generative AI: AI may assist research and drafting with a brief disclosure; the analysis, arguments, and conclusions you submit must be your own.

> [!note]- Quizzes Rubric
> | Criterion | Unacceptable — 0% | Minimal — 70% | Competent — 85% | Effective — 90% | Mastery — 100% |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | Business Argument & Reasoning (55%) | No argument. The answer summarises or restates material without taking or defending a position; reasoning is absent, circular, or incoherent. | A position is stated but thinly reasoned. The "why" is asserted rather than argued, and the logic has visible gaps. | A clear position with generally sound reasoning. The argument holds together, though depth or the handling of trade-offs is limited. | A well-reasoned business argument that makes its logic explicit, weighs trade-offs, and reaches a defensible conclusion. | A sharp argument that reasons from evidence to a decision an executive could act on, anticipates the strongest counter-case, and resolves it. |
> | Evidence: Cases & Frameworks (30%) | No specific course case or framework is used, or references are incorrect or misapplied. | A case or framework is named but used loosely or only as a label; it does not carry the argument. | At least one specific case and framework are applied correctly and support the claim. | Cases and frameworks are chosen deliberately and integrated so the argument depends on them. | Evidence is marshalled precisely — the right case and framework for the point — showing command of the module's material. |
> | Structure & Executive Readability (15%) | Disorganised or far outside the length guidance; hard to follow. | A discernible structure but choppy transitions, or notably over/under the 400–600-word range. | Clear structure within range; readable and mostly well-ordered. | Logically structured, concise, and easy for a busy reader to follow. | Tightly organised and persuasive; every paragraph earns its place within the length budget. |

## 2 · Discussion Forums (30% — 10% each)

Three forums, one at the end of Modules 1, 2, and 3. Your original post is approximately 300–350 words and is graded on depth of analysis, not length. You also respond substantively to two peer posts per forum.

Substantive engagement means challenging an assumption with evidence, adding a real case or data point, or offering a reasoned alternative. Agreement, restatement, and generic observation do not count.

> [!note]- Discussion Forums Rubric
> | Criterion | Unacceptable — 0% | Minimal — 70% | Competent — 85% | Effective — 90% | Mastery — 100% |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | Original Post: Analytical Argument (50%) | A summary of readings or a generic opinion; no argument, no counterargument, and not actionable. | An argument is attempted but stays close to summary; reasoning is thin and no limitation or counter-view is acknowledged. | A clear analytical argument that goes beyond summary and reads as usable, though the counterargument or limitation is treated lightly. | A well-reasoned argument that acknowledges a real counterargument or limitation and gives an executive something to act on. | A crisp argument that advances a defensible position, engages the strongest objection, and lands as executive-ready analysis. |
> | Original Post: Grounding in Cases & Frameworks (20%) | No specific reference to a course case or framework. | A vague or incidental reference that does not shape the argument. | At least one specific, correct reference that supports the point. | References are chosen well and integrated into the reasoning. | Evidence is precise and connective, showing command of the module. |
> | Substantive Peer Engagement (two responses) (30%) | Responses missing, or replies that only agree, restate, or offer generic observations. | One substantive response, or two that only partly clear the bar (mostly affirmation with little new evidence). | Two responses that each add something — a case, a data point, or a reasoned alternative. | Two responses that challenge an assumption with evidence or offer a reasoned alternative that advances the thread. | Two responses that reshape the discussion, introducing evidence or a reframing the original author would have to reckon with. |

## 3 · Final Presentation (20%)

Board-style team presentation of your Intelligent Enterprise Blueprint on December 11 — 20 minutes of presentation followed by 5 minutes of Q&A, treated as a board deciding whether to fund your roadmap. All team members are expected to present. Where possible, an external practitioner panel evaluates in a competition format. Graded as a team.

> [!note]- Final Presentation Rubric
> | Criterion | Unacceptable — 0% | Minimal — 70% | Competent — 85% | Effective — 90% | Mastery — 100% |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | Strategic Argument & Reasoning Under Questioning (50%) | No coherent investment case; the team cannot explain or defend its choices when questioned. | A basic case is presented but reasoning is shallow; Q&A answers are evasive, off-point, or unsupported. | A sound investment case, defended reasonably under questioning, though depth or composure varies. | A compelling case defended with clear reasoning; Q&A answers are direct, evidence-based, and hold up. | A board-ready argument; under pressure the team reasons openly, concedes fairly, and strengthens its case. |
> | Solution Design & Governance Quality (25%) | Design is absent, incoherent, or ungoverned, with no credible link to business outcomes. | A design exists but is weakly connected to outcomes, with governance an afterthought. | A workable design tied to business outcomes, with governance addressed. | A strong, coherent design whose value, cost, and risk are argued and whose governance is deliberate. | An elegant end-to-end design — diagnosis through roadmap — with governance-by-design and a clear cash/value argument. |
> | Visual Presentation & Board Professionalism (15%) | Slides are cluttered or illegible; the deck detracts; time is badly mismanaged. | Basic slides with readability or consistency problems; loose timing. | Clear, board-appropriate slides; time largely respected. | Clean, well-designed deck that supports the argument; disciplined 20+5 timing. | A polished, board-grade deck that advances the argument; timing is precise and confident. |

## 4 · Executive Board Memo (20%)

A 4–5 page professional board document due December 14, built progressively from your four lab artifacts and structured in seven sections: Executive Summary; Context & Background; Integration Backbone Assessment; Automation Journey; Agent Design & Governance; Three-Phase Roadmap; and Human-AI Partnership. It should reflect the full journey, including insights from the December 11 presentation and other teams' Q&A. Graded as a team.

> [!note]- Executive Board Memo Rubric
> | Criterion | Unacceptable — 0% | Minimal — 70% | Competent — 85% | Effective — 90% | Mastery — 100% |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | Strategic Argument & Business Case (45%) | No coherent case; recommendations are unsupported or absent, with no link to value. | A case is present but reasoning is thin; the roadmap lacks credible value, cost, or risk. | A sound investment case with a three-phase roadmap carrying reasonable value, cost, and risk. | A persuasive case that argues how the work moves cash conversion, working capital, or revenue — not cost alone. | A board-grade argument a team could take to a board: value-led, trade-offs faced, roadmap credible and financed. |

## 6 · Team Engagement — Peer Evaluation (5%)

Set by confidential peer evaluation rather than the instructor's guess at who did what. There are two rounds: a formative round at the midterm (no grade weight — to surface problems while they are still fixable) and a graded round at the end of term. Each member rates every teammate, and themselves, on the three dimensions below with a one-line comment.

The default is the full 5%. A member who carried more than their share is never penalised. Individual ratings are visible only to the instructor, who holds final judgment and discusses any downward adjustment with the student before applying it.

### What each member is rated on

| Dimension | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Reliability | Follows through on commitments; work arrives on time and as agreed. |
| Contribution to Deliverables | Does a fair share of the substantive lab and memo work. |
| Collaboration | Communicates, supports teammates, and engages constructively. |

Each dimension is rated on a short scale each round — 4 = exceeds expectations, 3 = meets, 2 = below, 1 = consistently below — with a one-line comment.

### How the end-of-term rating sets the mark

| Outcome | Trigger | Mark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full credit | Ratings place the member at "meets" or above — the default for any functioning team. | 5% of 5% |
| Minor reduction | Isolated but corroborated concern on one dimension, consistent with the team charter, short of substantial under-contribution. | ~4% of 5% |
| Substantial reduction | Peer ratings consistently and substantially flag under-contribution across teammates, corroborated and consistent with the charter's non-contribution terms. | ~3.5% of 5% |
| Major reduction | Near-total disengagement, corroborated across teammates and documented against the charter. | 0–2.5% of 5% |

Every reduction requires corroboration across teammates, consistency with the team charter, and a conversation with the student first. Over-contribution is never penalised.
