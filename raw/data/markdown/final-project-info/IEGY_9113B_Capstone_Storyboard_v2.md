## How to Read and Use This Storyboard

> [!info]- Purpose
> The capstone spine. It maps the 14 lessons to the final board presentation so students see the end state from Week 1, and each week's homework produces a defined piece of the final deck. It also serves as the deliverables-to-project map: every graded deliverable is tagged to its origin — in-class work, homework/teamwork, or lab.

> [!info]- The three phases = existing milestones (not a new framework)
> Diagnose → Design → Implement is the plain-language gloss on the course's existing Milestones 1–2–3: M1 "What is the problem?" (W1–W6, Labs 1–2), M2 "What is the design to close it?" (W7–W10, Lab 3), M3 "How do we manage what we built?" (W11–W14, Lab 4 + final). No new construct was invented.

> [!info]- Board-deck sizing
> Target ~13 content slides for the 20-minute board presentation (~60–90 sec each), plus 5 min Q&A. The slide count mirrors the 7 Board-Memo sections so the deck and the memo are the same argument. Many lessons feed one slide — the storyboard maps lessons-to-slides many-to-one, never one slide per lesson. Choosing what earns a slide is itself the board discipline students practise.

> [!warning]- Two different three-part structures — don't conflate
> - **Project method** (this storyboard's spine): Diagnose → Design → Implement — how the TEAM works through the semester.
> - **Roadmap** (inside Milestone 3, slides S10–S11): Stabilise → Automate → Orchestrate — what the COMPANY does over 36 months.
