These rubrics apply to the six graded components of IE-GY 9113B.
How these rubrics work
Each criterion below is scored on five levels — Unacceptable (0%), Minimal (70%), Competent (85%), Effective (90%), and Mastery (100%). A component’s score is the
weighted sum of its criteria; component scores then combine according to the grade breakdown and map to the letter scale, both shown on this page. Five principles run
through every rubric:
1. Reasoning is the primary graded objective. Across quizzes, discussion forums, in-class discussion, and the final presentation, marks are earned by explaining and
defending the reasoning behind a business decision — why this diagnosis, why this design, why this trade-off — not by summarising a reading or reciting a framework.
2. Arguments, not summaries. Every written and spoken deliverable is assessed as a business argument: a claim, the evidence for it, and a defensible conclusion an
executive could act on.
3. Cases and frameworks must do work. Claims are grounded in specific course cases and named frameworks, used to advance the argument rather than as
decoration.
4. Platform-neutral. Work derived from the labs is graded on business outcomes, governance thinking, and solution-design quality — never on the platform chosen or its
complexity. The Default no-code stack earns full marks.
5. Synthetic data only. All project data is synthetic; no confidential company information is used or required. Governance and risk thinking are assessed on the quality of
the design, not on access to real data.

6 · Team Engagement — Peer Evaluation (5%)
Set by confidential peer evaluation rather than the instructor’s guess at who did what. There are two rounds: a formative round at the midterm (no grade weight — to surface
problems while they are still fixable) and a graded round at the end of term. Each member rates every teammate, and themselves, on the three dimensions below with a one-line
comment.
The default is the full 5%. A member who carried more than their share is never penalised. Individual ratings are visible only to the instructor, who holds final judgment and
discusses any downward adjustment with the student before applying it.
What each member is rated on
Dimension What it means
Reliability Follows through on commitments; work arrives on time and as agreed.
Contribution to Deliverables Does a fair share of the substantive lab and memo work.
Collaboration Communicates, supports teammates, and engages constructively.
Each dimension is rated on a short scale each round — 4 = exceeds expectations, 3 = meets, 2 = below, 1 = consistently below — with a one-line comment.
How the end-of-term rating sets the mark
Outcome Trigger Mark
Full credit Ratings place the member at “meets” or above — the default for any functioning team. 5% of 5%

Substantial reduction Peer ratings consistently and substantially flag under-contribution across teammates, corroborated ~3.5% of 5%

Major reduction Near-total disengagement, corroborated across teammates and documented against the charter. 0–2.5% of 5%
Every reduction requires corroboration across teammates, consistency with the team charter, and a conversation with the student first. Over-contribution is never penalised.
